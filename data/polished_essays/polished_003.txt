My grandmother measures flour by hand rather than with a cup, and for years I found myself unable to replicate her bread no matter how diligently I tried. She would observe me kneading and simply shake her head with quiet disapproval. One memorable Sunday, she finally placed her hands over mine instead of offering further explanation, and something meaningful clicked into place that no recipe card could ever convey. Now, when relatives visit, I am the one preparing the dough while she critiques the crust from her chair with characteristic candor. In today's society, such traditions are increasingly rare. It is not truly about the bread itself; rather, it is the one time we are all quiet together in the same room, united by something timeless.
