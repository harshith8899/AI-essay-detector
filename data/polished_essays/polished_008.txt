I relocated here at eleven years old and comprehended perhaps one word out of every ten during my first English class. I would diligently write phrases phonetically in the back of my notebook simply to navigate lunch conversations successfully. My teacher, Ms. Reyes, permitted me to answer questions in writing before I could articulate them aloud, a small accommodation that proved profoundly significant. By eighth grade, I was translating for my mother during parent-teacher conferences. I continue to think in two languages depending on the subject matter, and I have stopped apologizing for the accent that emerges when I am fatigued, embracing it instead as part of my identity.
